Effective as of 1 November 2026 · Download PDF

Spend Brake Data Processing Agreement. The current version is published at https://spendbrake.dev/dpa.

In short

  • When your organization sends personal data through the hosted Spend Brake service, your organization is the controller and I, Kristiyan Ivanov, am its processor. This agreement sets out the terms Article 28 GDPR requires for that.
  • It forms part of the Terms of Service and applies automatically when you use the Hosted Service. No signature is needed; if your records need a countersigned copy, email me and I will return one.
  • Request and response bodies and query strings pass through the proxy and are never stored. What is stored is metadata about each call, your team and your configuration, in DigitalOcean's Frankfurt region, with the security measures in Annex II.
  • Sub-processors are listed in Annex III. I will tell you 30 days before adding one, and you may object.
  • I will tell you about a personal data breach affecting your data without undue delay and in any case within 48 hours of becoming aware of it.
  • A self-hosted install is not covered: I process nothing for you there.

1. Definitions

  • “Data Protection Law” means Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (the “GDPR”), the GDPR as it forms part of the law of the United Kingdom (the “UK GDPR”) and the UK Data Protection Act 2018, the Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection, and any national law implementing or supplementing them, in each case as they apply to the processing under this DPA.
  • “Customer Data” means personal data that you, your applications, agents or team members send to or through the Hosted Service, as described in Annex I. It includes the contents of proxied requests while they pass through the proxy, the usage events and server logs the Hosted Service records about them, and the team-member, alert-recipient and configuration data you enter.
  • “Hosted Service” has the meaning given in the Terms: the hosted Spend Brake service at app.spendbrake.dev, including its proxy endpoints, dashboard, admin API and the brake command-line tool when it talks to it.

2. Scope and roles

2.1 You are the controller, I am the processor

For Customer Data, you are the controller (or a processor acting for your own customers, in which case you warrant that your instructions to me are covered by their instructions to you) and I am your processor. I process Customer Data only to provide the Hosted Service to you under the Terms and this DPA.

2.2 What this DPA does not cover

  • Data for which I am the controller. The email address and name you use to open an account, billing data, support correspondence, and analytics about the use of the website and dashboard are processed by me as controller, as described in the Privacy Policy. Where the same piece of data serves both purposes (for example a team member's email address, which I need to sign them in and which you entered to give them access to your org), this DPA governs my processing of it on your instructions and the Privacy Policy governs my own.
  • API providers. Every request through the proxy is forwarded to the provider you addressed (for example Google Maps Platform or Twilio) with your own provider key, exactly as your application sent it. The provider processes it under your agreement with them. It is not my Sub-processor, and I do not control what it does with the request.
  • Destinations you configure. Alerts, webhooks, emails and OpenTelemetry spans go to the Slack, Discord, Microsoft Teams, webhook, email or OTLP endpoints you set up. Sending them is your instruction; the recipients are your processors or third parties, not mine.
  • Self-hosted installs. If you run the Spend Brake software on your own infrastructure, I do not process any data for you and this DPA does not apply. A Business or Enterprise License is verified offline and sends nothing to me.

3. Details of the processing

The subject matter, duration, nature and purpose of the processing, the categories of data subjects and the categories of personal data are set out in Annex I. In outline: your applications and agents send API requests to the proxy; the proxy prices each request, checks it against your caps, forwards it to the provider with your key, streams the response back and records metadata about the call. Request and response bodies and query strings are handled in memory only and are never stored.

Annex I: Details of processing

Parties

  • Controller (data exporter): the Customer, the organization holding the org on the Hosted Service, acting through its owners.
  • Processor (data importer): Kristiyan Ivanov, self-employed individual registered in Bulgaria (BULSTAT 180490275), zh.k. Hadzhi Dimitar 16, entrance B, floor 1, apt. 13, Poduyane district, 1510 Sofia, Bulgaria, kristiyan@spendbrake.dev.

Subject matter

Operation of the Hosted Service for the Customer: a proxy that enforces spend caps on the paid APIs the Customer's applications and agents call, with the dashboard, admin API, alerts, telemetry and command-line tool around it.

Duration

For as long as the Customer holds an org on the Hosted Service, plus the deletion period in Section 9.2 of the DPA.

Nature of the processing

  • Receiving each request the Customer's applications and agents send to the proxy; reading its route and parameters in memory to price it, to check it against caps, to detect identical repeated calls (by a one-way hash) and to enforce IP allowlists; forwarding it to the API provider the Customer addressed with the Customer's own provider key; streaming the provider's response back to the Customer without caching it.
  • Recording a usage event with metadata about each call; keeping request logs; storing the Customer's configuration, encrypted provider credentials, team members and audit log; showing all of this in the dashboard, the admin API, Prometheus metrics and CSV exports; sending the alerts, webhooks, emails and OpenTelemetry spans the Customer configures.
  • Hosting, backing up and deleting the above.

Purpose

To enforce the spend caps, loop rules and kill switches the Customer sets; to account for and show what each call cost; to alert the Customer; and otherwise to provide the Hosted Service as described in the Terms. No other purpose.

Categories of data subjects

  • People whose data is in proxied requests: the Customer's end users, customers, contacts and other persons whose data the Customer's applications and agents include in API requests, for example an address in a geocoding request, a place search, or a phone number and message in a Twilio request. This data is processed in transit only.
  • People and systems calling the proxy: the Customer's developers, staff, automated agents and, where the Customer calls the proxy from end-user devices, its end users, whose client IP address, user agent and trace context are seen by the proxy.
  • The Customer's team and contacts: owners, admins and viewers the Customer invites to its org, people named in the audit log, and the recipients of the alert emails the Customer configures.

Categories of personal data

  • In transit, never stored: request bodies, response bodies and query strings of proxied calls, which may contain whatever the Customer's applications put in them. They are held in memory for the duration of the call and are not written to the database, logs, traces or caches.
  • Usage events: timestamp; org, key, environment, agent and session labels (free text chosen by the Customer, which may identify a person if the Customer puts a name or id there); provider, route and SKU; worst-case and actual cost; whether the route was underpriced; outcome (allowed, blocked, loop detected, upstream error); the provider's HTTP status and latency; the caller's trace context (traceparent), user agent, and request and response sizes in bytes; and a one-way hash of the request that cannot be turned back into it.
  • Server logs: HTTP method, URL with secret query parameters redacted, the client IP address as seen by the proxy (behind the HTTPS front, the forwarded address), status code and timing. Client IP addresses are also used transiently to rate-limit sign-in and admin endpoints and to check IP allowlists; they are not part of usage events.
  • Team and configuration: team members' email addresses (stored lower-case) and roles; hashed sign-in and API tokens; audit log entries naming who changed caps, keys, credentials, team members and kill switches, and when; key names, caps, environments, loop rules and IP allowlists; alert email addresses; webhook URLs, Slack, Discord and Teams webhooks, and OpenTelemetry endpoints and headers (webhook URLs and telemetry headers stored encrypted); the Customer's provider API keys, stored encrypted.

Special categories of data

None intended. The Hosted Service does not store request contents and is not designed for special categories of personal data or data about criminal convictions; the Customer must not put such data in stored configuration (Section 11 of the DPA).

Frequency, retention and location

  • Frequency: continuous, for every request made with one of the Customer's Spend Brake keys and every action in the dashboard or admin API.
  • Retention: usage events for the Customer's plan's retention period (7 days on Free and Indie, 30 days on Team, 365 days on Agents); server logs 14 days; team, keys and configuration while the org exists; provider credentials until the Customer removes them or deletes the org; database backups 14 days.
  • Location: DigitalOcean's Frankfurt, Germany (FRA1) region, with the Sub-processors in Annex III for the functions stated there.

Annex II: Technical and organisational measures

These are the measures in place as of the effective date, written from the code, which is public at github.com/KIvanow/spend-brake. The security page describes them in more detail and names the files that implement them. Spend Brake has no SOC 2 or ISO 27001 certification.

Encryption of secrets at rest

  • Provider API keys, webhook URLs and their signing secrets, OpenTelemetry headers, SSO client secrets and billing-export service accounts are sealed with envelope encryption: a fresh random 32-byte data key per secret, used once with AES-256-GCM and a random 12-byte nonce, then wrapped by a master key held in the service's environment or in AWS KMS. Only the wrapped data key is stored.
  • Both layers carry associated data naming the purpose, the org id and the credential id, so a ciphertext moved to another org or row fails to decrypt. Data keys are zeroed in memory after use.
  • Plaintext secrets exist only in the proxy's memory (cached for at most five minutes) and, for Twilio price lookups, in the worker's. Nothing writes a decrypted key to disk, to a log, to a trace or to a response. The master key is not part of any backup.

Encryption in transit

  • The hosted proxy and dashboard sit behind Caddy, which obtains and renews certificates from Let's Encrypt. Both send Strict-Transport-Security: max-age=31536000; includeSubDomains. Server version headers are removed.
  • Requests to API providers are forwarded over HTTPS with the Customer's key injected by the proxy; the credential headers the Customer's application sent (Authorization, Cookie, X-Goog-Api-Key, X-Api-Key, X-Subscription-Token, X-Brake-*) are dropped before forwarding.

Data minimisation

  • Request bodies, response bodies and query strings are never stored. Responses are streamed through and not cached. Only the metadata listed in Annex I is recorded.
  • A redacting logger replaces credential headers with [redacted], strips the values of secret query parameters (key, api_key, apikey, access_token, token, signature, client_secret) and removes anything shaped like a Spend Brake key from free text, including error messages, before a log line is written.
  • Product analytics on the dashboard are linked to internal user and org ids, never to email addresses or names, contain no key values, credentials, amounts or request content, and the PostHog project is set to discard IP addresses. No session recordings, surveys or autocapture.

Access control and authentication

  • Spend Brake keys and admin API tokens are stored as SHA-256 hashes; keys are shown once when created, can be scoped, capped, restricted to an IP allowlist and revoked at any time; tokens can carry an expiry and be revoked.
  • Dashboard sign-in is by one-time emailed link carrying a random 32-byte code in the URL fragment (so it does not appear in server logs); only the code's hash is stored, it is valid for 15 minutes and works once; at most five links an hour are sent to one address; a session lasts seven days. There are no passwords. Orgs may instead use single sign-on (OIDC, SAML) with a Business or Enterprise License.
  • Org roles (owner, admin, viewer; with more roles under a License) limit what each team member can do. Admin endpoints are rate-limited per client address; behind the HTTPS front only the private hops in front of the service are trusted to say what that address is.
  • Operationally, one person, the Processor, administers the Hosted Service. No employees or contractors have access to production systems or Customer Data.

Integrity and accountability

  • Changes to caps, keys, credentials, team members and kill switches are recorded in an audit log with who made them and when; the audit log can be exported as CSV.
  • Outgoing webhooks and alert action links (Kill, Raise, Mute) are signed with HMAC-SHA256 over a timestamp and the raw body, so recipients can verify them.
  • Every cap check is one atomic step in the budget store; if the store is unavailable the proxy fails closed (HTTP 503) unless a key has opted into a bounded fail-open allowance.

Network and platform security

  • Outbound connections to URLs the Customer supplies (webhooks, OpenTelemetry collectors, custom APIs, SSO issuers) go through a guarded HTTP client that refuses loopback, private, link-local (including cloud metadata), carrier-grade NAT, multicast, unspecified, NAT64 and IPv4-mapped addresses, checked on the address actually connected to.
  • Postgres and Valkey are not published outside the service's container network; the application ports bind to the loopback interface and are reached only through Caddy.
  • The dashboard is served with a Content Security Policy that allows scripts and styles from itself only, connections to its own API and PostHog EU, and forbids framing. The website has a stricter policy still and sends X-Content-Type-Options, X-Frame-Options and Referrer-Policy headers.

Availability, backups and retention

  • The Hosted Service, its database and cache run on DigitalOcean in the Frankfurt, Germany (FRA1) region.
  • Backups of Postgres (configuration, ledger, audit log) and Valkey (live counters, reservations, kill switches) are taken nightly and kept for 14 days. They are stored on the same server as the service, contain the encrypted credential envelopes and not the master key, and are used only to recover the service.
  • Usage events are deleted automatically after the plan's retention period by a cleanup job that runs every six hours; server logs are kept 14 days.

Incident handling and transparency

  • Personal data breaches affecting Customer Data are notified to the Customer without undue delay and in any case within 48 hours of my becoming aware of them (Section 8 of the DPA).
  • Security problems can be reported to kristiyan@spendbrake.dev, which I read myself. The whole codebase, including the proxy, the budget engine, the encryption and the infrastructure files, is public, so the measures above can be verified by reading it.
