Privacy Policy

Effective as of 31 October 2026

In short

  • Spend Brake is run by me, Kristiyan Ivanov, an individual, not a company.
  • I store your account email, your provider API keys (encrypted), and metadata about each proxied call: what it cost, not what it said. Request and response bodies and query strings are never stored.
  • Payments go through Stripe. Transactional email goes through Resend. The service runs on DigitalOcean; this website is served by Vercel. Product analytics go to PostHog, hosted in the EU.
  • I do not sell personal information. Neither this website nor the dashboard sets cookies or keeps anything in your browser for analytics; they count page views and button clicks to see how Spend Brake is used.
  • A self-hosted install sends nothing to me.

Spend Brake (“Spend Brake,” “I,” “me,” or “we”) is a proxy that enforces spend caps on paid APIs. It is operated by Kristiyan Ivanov, an individual (“the operator”). This Privacy Policy describes how I process personal information collected through the website at spendbrake.dev, the hosted service at app.spendbrake.dev and its proxy endpoints, the brake command-line tool when it talks to the hosted service, and emails I send (collectively, the “Service”).

The Service is intended for businesses and developers. When an organization (“org”) on the Service invites you as a team member, or routes its applications and agents through its account, that org decides what is sent through the proxy. If you have concerns about information an org processes through Spend Brake, please direct them to that org first.

Self-hosted installs. If you run Spend Brake on your own servers, the software stores everything described below on your infrastructure, not mine. It does not report usage, telemetry or account data to me; license files are verified offline. Only the following reach anything outside your install: price checks fetch public provider price pages; the optional signed catalog feed downloads price bundles from the feed URL you configure; and any alert, email, OpenTelemetry or webhook destinations you set up receive what you send them. Its dashboard sends product analytics only if you turn them on with your own PostHog project. This policy therefore applies to self-hosted installs only to the extent you contact me, buy a license, or use the website.

Personal Information I Collect

Information you provide

  • Account data: the email address you sign up with, the name you give your org or project, your role in the org (owner, admin or viewer), and the email addresses of people you invite. Emails are stored lower-case so sign-in can find them. Sign-in uses one-time emailed links, so there is no password; instead the Service stores hashed sign-in and API tokens.
  • Provider credentials: the API keys you store for providers such as Google Maps Platform, Twilio, Mapbox, HERE, Tavily, Exa, Brave or SerpApi. They are encrypted at rest with envelope encryption under a master key, decrypted in memory only to forward your requests, and never written to logs or traces.
  • Configuration: your Spend Brake keys, caps, environments, agent and session labels, loop rules, IP allowlists you set on keys, alert thresholds, and the destinations you configure for alerts and telemetry (webhook URLs, Slack, Discord or Teams webhooks, alert email addresses, OpenTelemetry endpoints and headers). Webhook URLs and OpenTelemetry headers are stored encrypted.
  • Payment data. If you buy a plan or a license, your payment information (card number, billing address and related details) is collected and processed directly by my payment processor, Stripe. I do not receive or store your full card number. I receive limited data from Stripe such as your Stripe customer id, plan, subscription and invoice status, and may see the card type and last four digits, billing name and address in Stripe's dashboard for support and accounting. See Stripe's Privacy Policy.
  • Communications data from our exchanges, such as when you email me for support.

Information collected when requests go through the proxy

For every call made with one of your Spend Brake keys, the Service records a usage event containing:

  • timestamp, org, key, environment, agent and session labels;
  • provider, route and SKU(s) the request was priced as;
  • worst-case and actual cost in micro-dollars, whether the route was underpriced, and the outcome (allowed, blocked, loop detected, upstream error);
  • the provider's HTTP status and latency;
  • the caller's trace context (traceparent), user agent, and request and response sizes in bytes;
  • a one-way hash of the request used to detect identical repeated calls; it cannot be turned back into the request.

What is never stored: request bodies, response bodies, query strings, the places, addresses or messages your requests were about, and the provider's responses. Responses are streamed through to your application and not cached. The dashboard, the ledger, Prometheus metrics and OpenTelemetry spans are all built from the metadata above.

Automatic data collection

  • Server logs. The proxy and admin API write request logs containing the HTTP method, the URL with secret query parameters redacted, the client IP address as seen by the proxy (behind the HTTPS front, the forwarded address), status code and timing. Credential headers are censored before anything is written. Logs are kept for 14 days.
  • IP addresses are also used transiently to rate-limit sign-in and admin endpoints, and are compared against an IP allowlist if you set one on a key. They are not part of usage events.
  • Audit log. Changes to caps, keys, credentials, team members and kill switches are recorded with who made them and when, so your org can see what happened.
  • This website (spendbrake.dev) is static. It sets no cookies. Vercel, which serves it, keeps its own request logs (including IP addresses) under Vercel's Privacy Policy. Its one third-party script, PostHog's analytics library, is loaded from the jsDelivr content delivery network, which sees your IP address when it serves the file.
  • The dashboard (app.spendbrake.dev) keeps your sign-in token in your browser's local or session storage so you stay signed in. It uses no tracking cookies.
  • Product analytics. The website and the dashboard send usage events to PostHog so I can see which pages and features are used and where people get stuck. They record:
    • the pages you view (on the dashboard, the page name only, never the address bar's sign-in codes or tokens) and the site that referred you;
    • on the website, clicks on the sign-up, sign-in, documentation, GitHub and email links (which plan, where on the page) and which sections you scroll to;
    • on the dashboard, that you signed up (and the plan), added a provider key (which provider), created a Spend Brake key, set a cap (the kind of scope only, such as org or key), added a custom API, started a checkout (the plan) or exported a CSV;
    • browser, operating system, device type and screen size, and a coarse location (country and city) that PostHog derives from your IP address. Spend Brake's PostHog project is set to discard IP addresses rather than store them.
    On the website you are not identified at all. On the dashboard, once you sign in, events are linked to your internal user id and your org's id, never to your email address or name. The events never contain key values, credentials, email addresses, amounts or the content of your requests. Neither site sets cookies or uses local or session storage for analytics, and there are no session recordings, surveys or automatic capture of what you type or click.
  • Email. Transactional emails (sign-in links, invitations, alerts, license files, receipts) are sent through Resend. I do not embed tracking pixels, and I do not use open or click tracking.

  • Process payments through Stripe and send related information such as receipts and license files;
  • Send the alerts you configure (thresholds, kills, loops, price changes, upstream key errors) to the destinations you choose;
  • Enforce plan limits, detect loops and runaway spend, and keep the Service secure;
  • Communicate with you about the Service: security notices, changes to these policies, support and administrative messages;
  • Respond to your requests, questions and feedback.

Compliance and protection

  • Protect my, your or others' rights, privacy, safety or property, including by making and defending legal claims;
  • Enforce the Terms of Service;
  • Prevent, identify, investigate and deter fraudulent, harmful, unauthorized or illegal activity, including abuse of the proxy or of the providers behind it.

I do not use your data to train models, to build advertising profiles, or for marketing to third parties.

  • Service providers that work on my behalf and may process personal information only to provide their service to me:
    Provider Purpose What they see
    Stripe Payments, subscriptions, invoices Billing details, email, plan
    Resend Transactional email Recipient email, message content (sign-in links, alerts, invitations, licenses)

Data Retention

  • Usage events are deleted automatically after your plan's retention period: 7 days on Free and Indie, 30 days on Team, 365 days on Agents. The cleanup job runs every six hours.
  • Account, team, keys and configuration are kept while your account exists. Removed team members are marked removed and can no longer sign in, but their entry is kept so the audit log still names who did what.
  • Provider credentials are deleted when you remove them or delete the org.
  • Payment records at Stripe and in my accounting are kept as long as tax and accounting law requires.
  • Analytics events are kept in PostHog only as long as they help me understand how the Service is used, under the retention settings of my PostHog project. On request I will delete the events linked to your account.
  • Server logs: 14 days. Database backups: 14 days; deleted data may persist in backups until they rotate.

When I no longer need personal information, I delete or anonymize it. To delete your account and its data, email me (see below).

Security

Provider keys, webhook URLs and telemetry headers are encrypted at rest; the real provider key is decrypted only in the proxy's memory and never logged. Secrets are stripped from log lines and traces. Traffic to and from the Service uses HTTPS. The database and cache are reachable only from the Service's own containers. Still, security risk is inherent in all internet and information technologies, and I cannot guarantee the security of your personal information.
