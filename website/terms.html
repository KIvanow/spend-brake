5. How Caps Work and What Is Not Guaranteed

Spend caps are the point of Spend Brake, so this section states plainly what they do and do not do.

  • Enforced before forwarding. Each request is priced at its worst case from Spend Brake's price catalog, reserved against every applicable cap in one atomic step, and refused with HTTP 402 if any cap would be crossed. The actual cost is settled afterwards. Overshoot past a cap is therefore bounded by one request's worst-case price per concurrent request.
  • Prices come from the providers' public price lists and are maintained by hand in data files that cite their source. Providers change prices, add SKUs and apply discounts, free tiers, commitments and taxes that Spend Brake may not know about. Spend Brake checks provider price pages for drift and alerts you, but a catalog may lag or be wrong. Your provider's bill is the authoritative record of what you spent.
  • Caps only hold if the proxy is the only path to spend. If a provider key is used anywhere other than through Spend Brake, or is not restricted to the proxy's egress addresses and the APIs you use, Spend Brake cannot see or stop that spend. You are responsible for restricting your keys and for keeping the provider's own budgets and quotas on as a backstop.
  • Unpriced routes are refused, not allowed. If a request cannot be priced it is blocked with HTTP 402 unpriced_route.
  • Failure mode. If the budget store is unavailable, the proxy fails closed and refuses requests with HTTP 503, unless a key has opted into a fail-open allowance, in which case spend up to that allowance may occur without a live check.
  • Loop detection and kill switches act on patterns and commands you configure. They are safeguards, not guarantees that an agent or application cannot misbehave in other ways.
  • Availability. When the Hosted Service is down, requests through it fail; they are not forwarded. Design your applications to handle 402, 429 and 503 responses.

Accordingly, I do not guarantee that your provider bill will not exceed any cap you set, and provider charges are excluded from my liability under Section 13.
